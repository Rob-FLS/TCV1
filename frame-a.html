Companies whose AI ambition has outrun their ability to control it

Some of you are already deploying AI and agentic solutions, but can't fully explain what they're doing. Others haven't moved yet, because you don't have confidence in the processes and systems that surround these solutions. Either way, the gap is the same: ambition has moved faster than control.

The real risk is not just falling behind. It's building the wrong thing, faster; or staying still because you can't trust what you're building.

Research and analysis referenced throughout this page draws on Gartner, Deloitte, and McKinsey.

The pressure is real, and it's pulling you in three directions at once

Your organization wants AI deployed faster, to drive efficiency and stay ahead of competitors. Regulators want traceability and documented decision-making, with real consequences for lacking it. Your teams are caught in between, slowed by rework and shifting expectations instead of shipping value.1

Nearly three-quarters of enterprises have deployed or are actively deploying AI agents; fewer than one in four report having a mature model for governing them.2 If that gap sounds familiar, you're not alone: most organizations moving on AI right now are moving without the control to match it. That gap doesn't stay hidden. Gartner expects more than 40% of agentic AI projects to be canceled by the end of 2027, not for technical reasons, but because of escalating costs, unclear value, or inadequate risk controls.3

If you're the company that hasn't moved yet, this is likely why. The hesitation isn't a lack of ambition; it's the absence of a way to trust what you'd be deploying.

Speed and control were never actually opposed

Most leaders treat this as a trade-off: move fast and lose control, or govern tightly and lose speed. That's a false choice, and it's an expensive one to act on. The companies pulling ahead aren't the ones moving fastest; they're the ones moving with the most visibility into what their systems are doing and why.4

When decision context, constraints, and reasoning are embedded into the work itself, rather than reconstructed after the fact, teams spend less time clarifying, less time redoing work, and less time defending decisions under pressure. Time-to-market correlates with profit margin more strongly than almost any other execution measure; the friction that governance is supposed to add is usually smaller than the friction that its absence already causes.5

Control, done right, is not the brake. It's what lets you press the accelerator with confidence. Treat speed and control as a real tradeoff, and confidence is the casualty either way: you either move without it, or you don't move at all.

Value: Essential and Yet Elusive

Every initiative your organization pursues, whether it's a technology investment, a process change, or an AI project, should exist because it creates real value; too often, that's not why it gets funded. Urgency, competitive pressure, and compelling technology drive decisions more than a tested value case does.

AI makes this worse, not better. Investment keeps accelerating even as measurement lags: fewer than half of organizations investing in AI can track financial ROI from those initiatives, yet most plan to keep increasing spending anyway.6 Conviction is outrunning evidence.

Managing value has to be a discipline, not an assumption. Leaders must know an initiative is worth pursuing before they commit to it, and be able to prove that value after the fact.

Control: Necessary and Yet Absent

Value tells you you're building the right thing. Control tells you whether you can trust how it runs once it's built, and with agentic systems now making real decisions on your behalf, that trust has to be earned. To do this, organizations must incorporate visibility, traceability, and accountability into their operations, across both business and technology teams: visibility into what a system is doing and why, traceability connecting every decision to what it touched, and accountability assigning an owner to every action, human or system-initiated.

Most organizations deploying agentic systems today have none of this locked down. Roughly 80% lack the audit trails, real-time monitoring, and decision boundaries needed to explain what their agents are actually doing.7

Managing control has to be a discipline, not an afterthought. Leaders must be able to explain a decision as it happens, not reconstruct it after something goes wrong.

Value + Control: The Secret to Impact

Knowing an initiative is worth pursuing and knowing you can defend how it runs are two different disciplines, and most organizations only build one of them.

Value without control gets you a good idea nobody can account for once questions get asked. Control without value gets you a well-governed initiative that was never worth building. Neither one, on its own, produces impact.

Traisee exists to make sure you get both.

The gaps described above, in adoption, ROI measurement, and agentic oversight, are documented by Gartner, Deloitte, and McKinsey. What follows is how Traisee closes them.

How Traisee works

Traisee doesn't tell your organization what to build or decide for you. It works inside the processes your teams already run, capturing the decisions being made and keeping them connected as the work moves forward, so that what you need to explain a system later is already there, not reconstructed after the fact.

That means a status question gets answered from what actually happened, not from someone's memory of it. It means a decision made early in a project stays linked to everything downstream that depended on it, so when something needs to change, or something breaks, you can follow that thread in either direction instead of starting from scratch. And it means every action, whether a person took it or an agent did, carries who was behind it and who's responsible for what happens next.

This works the same way whether the project is fully manual, fully agentic, or somewhere in between. Traisee doesn't distinguish between the two; it cares that the trail stays intact either way.

One principle holds throughout: Traisee facilitates, documents, and surfaces. It never decides. Your teams make every call; Traisee makes sure the call, and the reasoning behind it, doesn't disappear.

The cost of waiting is not neutral

This gap doesn't stay quiet forever. AI governance has become the top challenge on the audit committee agenda, ahead of cybersecurity, and board-level oversight of AI risk has roughly tripled in two years.8 The pressure to explain what your systems are doing is shifting from a technical question to a governance one, and it's moving fast.

Whether you're already exposed or still on the sidelines, this gap doesn't close on its own. It gets more expensive to close the longer it's left alone.

Traisee is not an IT tool, and it's not another AI governance point solution

It doesn't compete with your project tools, your documentation platform, or your observability stack; it connects the decisions inside them. And unlike governance tools built around model-level risk scoring, Traisee works at the level of business intent: why a decision was made, what it touched, and who's accountable for it, all the way from idea to execution.
